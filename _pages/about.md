I am a Ph.D. candidate at the <a href='https://www.eee.hku.hk/'>Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering (ECE)</a>, the University of Hong Kong (HKU), supervised by <a href='https://web.ece.hku.hk/~wnlee/'>Prof. Wei-Ning Lee</a>.

My research focuses on Computer Vision, Multimodal Image Processing, Vision-Language Models, and AI Agents for Science. My recent work mainly contributes to the ultrasound imaging field, including image (and IQ signal) reconstruction, denoising, segmentation, disease diagnosis, and biomarker measurement.

## Research
### Ultrasound reconstruction and signal recovery

I develop learning-based methods for reconstructing high-quality ultrasound data from sparse or degraded measurements. My work on synthetic-aperture ultrasound investigates spatiotemporal IQ signal recovery from sparse transmissions, improving acquisition efficiency. I also propose self-supervised test-time training for single-shot ultrasound denoising, with an emphasis on robustness under distribution shifts.

### Medical image understanding and foundation models

I investigate anatomy-aware and modality-aware representations for generalizable medical image analysis. This includes developing foundation models for multimodal segmentation, as well as adapting VLMs to effectively understand cardiac ultrasound images for precise clinical interpretation. A central objective is to incorporate anatomical structure or imaging physics into model design, rather than treating medical images as generic visual inputs.

### Translational biomedical AI

I develop clinically oriented models to measure quantitative information from medical images or physiological signals. Applications include ultrasound-based musculoskeletal assessment, CT-based robotic surgery planning, MRI-based cancer segmentation, facial weakness assessment for rare myopathy, and electrocardio panorama synthesis. This research emphasizes interpretability and practical integration into imaging or intervention workflows.
